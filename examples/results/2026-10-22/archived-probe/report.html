What this establishes—and what it does not

  • Exploratory retrieval probe selected after inspecting a known omission; not an independently sampled benchmark.
  • The task is new. It is not the original AIME question, an AIME answer repair, or a production incident.
  • The two archived memories are unchanged. Three coarse fragments were manually defined around the known helper section.
  • A repair concerns exact retrieval of two defaults only, not mathematical correctness or general memory quality.
  • The model may guess the defaults even when absent; such an after-context pass must not be suppressed.
  • Three sequential repeats are descriptive, not a significance test. Filler is character-matched, not token-matched.
  • Evidence is specific to this task, evaluator, runner, snapshot, and append-at-end placement.
  • Candidates use whitespace-normalized substring matching, not semantic equivalence or contradiction detection.
  • Filler matches Python character count and newline positions, not model token count or semantic neutrality.
  • A 1-minimal repair means no single retained fragment can be removed at the chosen pass threshold; it is not a globally smallest repair.
  • Finite, sequential repeats and adaptive search do not establish statistical significance, reproducibility across seeds, or universal causality.
  • Completed arms are reused only within this run; repeats are separate calls, and stateful runners must be reset for each call.
  • Full restoration failure does not rule out other repairs, replacement rather than appending, or non-monotonic subsets.

Character-matched filler is not a token-matched placebo. Repeated observations are not a significance test, and 1-minimal is not globally minimum. Findings depend on the task, evaluator, runner, restoration position and sampled trials.
